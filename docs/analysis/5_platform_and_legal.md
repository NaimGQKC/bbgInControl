# Sparrow AI: platform policy and legal/compliance risk (26 Nov 2026)

Not legal advice. Sources: official pages were fetched 26 Nov 2026, but several fetches went through a summarizer; items marked **(summarized)** or **(unverified)** need a human re-read. Repo claims are from code I read (`app/guards.py`, `coldmsg.py`, `templates.py`, `prospecting.py`, `engine.py`, `web.py`, `legal.html`).

## 1. WhatsApp platform

**1.1 Opt-in (HIGH, blocks cold prospecting).** Meta's Business Messaging Policy (page dated 23 Sep 2026) says you may contact people only if "they have given you their mobile phone number or username" and "you have received opt-in permission ... confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages" ([whatsappbusiness.com/policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). The opt-in guide requires the opt-in to name the business and to comply with local law ([getting-opt-in](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in), cites the Nov 2024 policy; summarized). A number lifted from a portal ad through CASAFARI is not an opt-in to this agent. The risk page does not say "public numbers", but nothing in the policy has a public-listing exception (my reading). The repo's cold flow (`is_cold`, `coldmsg`) contradicts this by design. Consequence ladder: warning, 1/3-day template block, 5/7/30-day block, account lock, permanent disablement, triggered by spam, template misclassification and excessive negative feedback ([policy-enforcement](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/policy-enforcement); summarized). Whether a restriction hits the whole account (all numbers) is not stated there (unverified). Mitigation: treat cold sends as outside Meta policy unless a lawyer plus Meta support (Tech Provider contact) confirm otherwise; the only compliant cold route is a non-WhatsApp first touch (portal message, call where lawful, postal) that earns an opt-in. Human: accept or reject this platform risk for the agent.

**1.2 Template categories (LOW-MED).** Valuation offers and follow-ups as MARKETING and confirmations/reminders as UTILITY are right. Sparrow-number notices like `agent_live` ("every lead gets a WhatsApp in 30 seconds") are promotional enough that Meta may recategorize (unverified); misclassification is an enforcement trigger (1.1). Record the agent's opt-in to alerts in onboarding copy (not found in code).

**1.3 Pricing and who pays (MED).** Per-message pricing since 1 Jul 2025; Spain marketing rate rose 1 Jul 2026; from 1 Oct 2026 service messages above 1,000 per number per month and utility templates inside an open window are charged ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing); summarized). The AI's free-form replies are service messages, so a busy agent pays Meta per reply past 1,000. Embedded Signup via a Tech Provider: customer owns the assets, and "your onboarded business customers must add a payment method" (only Solution Partners share a credit line) ([ES overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/overview); summarized). So error 131042 is expected until the agent adds a payment method; it can also come from missing tax info or an unaccepted "MessagingFor" request (third-party guides, unverified: [GoHighLevel](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007936-error-131042-business-eligibility-payment-issue)). Code does detect 131042 after the fact (`engine.py` cause text). Mitigation: a go-live gate that sends a real utility template to the agent's own alert number and refuses `live` until it delivers.

**1.4 Limits and quality (LOW-MED).** Limits are per business portfolio, start at 250 unique users per rolling 24h, then 2,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 ([messaging-limits](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/messaging-limits); summarized). Sparrow's 3-10/day cap is far below, so the real constraint is quality, not tier. Quality is read only before scans and on Meta's webhook; `UNKNOWN`/`NA` count as healthy (`prospecting.QUALITY_OK`), and the 15% opt-out trigger needs at least 10 leads, which takes days at 3/day. Mitigation: also pause on any user-block signal Meta exposes and after the first 2 opt-outs in the first 10 sends.

**1.5 Coexistence (MED).** Official limits: app 2.24.17+; groups, disappearing and view-once messages, broadcast lists and calls unsupported; 180-day history sync; disconnect after about 14 days of primary-device inactivity ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users); summarized). No country list on that page; Spain works in the repo's own live test. Embedded Signup v2/v3 are deprecated 15 Oct 2026 (same source, ES overview); the repo uses a `config_id` and Graph `v23.0`: confirm it is v4 (unverified).

**1.6 AI chatbot rule (LOW).** Meta's AI-providers clause (effective 15 Jan 2026) bars general-purpose assistants, not sales/support bots ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/)). Keep the prompt refusing off-topic chat; describe the product as appointment booking in Meta review. The policy requires "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths"; G8 covers that.

**1.7 Agent's number (HIGH reputational).** The cold flow runs from the agent's real business number with Coexistence, so a block spiral hits their daily business. Protections that exist: caps 3/5/10 with ramp-up, quality auto-pause (never auto-resumes), no follow-ups, one soft-no, hard stops in code, hours 09-21 Mon-Sat, footer with BAJA. Gaps: no stop on first block signals (see 1.4); the risk text in `coldmsg.RISKS` does not say the number can be permanently lost for ordinary WhatsApp use (whether Coexistence app use survives a Cloud API restriction is unverified).

## 2. Spain / EU law

**2.1 LSSI art. 21 and GDPR basis (HIGH).** Art. 21.1 bans promotional messages by email "u otro medio de comunicación electrónica equivalente" not requested or expressly authorised; exception 21.2 needs a prior contractual relationship ([BOE LSSI](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758)). Art. 38.4.d makes it minor (up to 30,000 EUR); mass or "insistent or systematic" sending is serious (30,001-150,000 EUR) ([same, art. 38-39](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758)). The AEPD has fined WhatsApp advertising under art. 21.1 (5,000 EUR example, press summary, [economistjurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/actualidad-juridica/jurisprudencia/sancionada-con-5-000-euros-una-consultora-energetica-por-el-envio-constante-y-no-autorizado-de-publicidad-a-una-persona)). EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 **v2.0, adopted 10 Nov 2026**, para. 113: unsolicited direct marketing by "email, SMS, MMS, RCS, messenger and other kinds of similar applications can only take place with the prior consent", so Art. 6(1)(f) cannot be the basis ([EDPB PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-11/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_v2_en.pdf), read in full text). `legal.html` section 10 and `coldmsg.RISKS` rely on "interés legítimo", which this contradicts. Whether a first "are you still selling?" message is advertising is the lawyer's question; its footer plus the offer of a valuation points toward "promotional". Mitigation: switch the cold channel or obtain consent first; stop describing legitimate interest as sufficient.

**2.2 Exclusion lists (MED-HIGH).** LOPDGDD art. 23.4 requires direct marketers to consult published exclusion systems first, unless the person consented ([BOE LOPDGDD](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673)). Lista Robinson covers phone and SMS/MMS channels (Adigital, via AEPD documents); the newer Lista STOP Publicidad (applicable from 31 Jan 2025) includes WhatsApp/messaging apps, with a 30-day consultation validity ([CMS](https://cms.law/es/esp/publication/lista-stop-publicidad-que-deben-saber-las-empresas-que-hacen-publicidad-sobre-la-nueva-lista-de-exclusion-publicitaria-en-proteccion-de-datos); summarized). Code consults neither (only its own suppression list). The correct article numbers are 19 (contact data) and 23 (exclusion); art. 21 LOPDGDD is about mercantile operations, not marketing.

**2.3 Art. 14 information (MED).** The footer (assistant, agent, source, BAJA, link to the owners' section) is a sound layered notice, but the linked text is an unreviewed draft.

**2.4 Controller/processor split (MED-HIGH).** Sparrow pulls CASAFARI data with its own key, deduplicates, and runs a cross-agent suppression list in its own interest, which looks like Sparrow determining means and purposes, so joint or independent controller risk exists. The draft says Sparrow is a controller only for aggregates and the suppression list. The contract cannot override facts. Human: decide the role and fix the DPA.

**2.5 Lystos/CSV intake treated as warm (HIGH, code gap).** `guards.COLD_SOURCES = {"casafari"}`; `/intake/{key}` leads (the repo says it is used for Lystos exports) get `LEAD_FIRST_CONTACT` plus two marketing follow-ups, with no source footer, no art. 14 link, no cold ladder, and no check of the cross-agent suppression list. If those exports are FSBO listings (probably), they are cold in law. Mitigation: require an attestation per intake key ("these people asked to be contacted") and otherwise route as cold, with suppression applied.

**2.6 Opt-out hash (MED).** `suppress_hash` is unsalted SHA-256 with a fixed public prefix. Spanish mobile numbers have about 10^8 values, so it is trivially reversible; the AEPD/EDPS paper says such hashes are pseudonymisation, and risk grows when the input domain is known ([EDPS-AEPD hash paper](https://edps.europa.eu/data-protection/our-work/publications/papers/introduction-hash-function-personal-data_en)). The privacy text calls it "unidirectional". Mitigation: HMAC-SHA256 with a secret key held outside the DB; the legal basis (obligation to honour objection) is defensible. Also `lead_rejected` events keep raw phones forever.

**2.7 Retention and DSAR (MED).** Delete-on-expiry works (`cleanup_expired`) but leads that replied keep conversation text. `/eliminar-datos` is a static page with an email; no request tracker or one-month clock (GDPR Art. 12(3), unverified here), and no operator tool to erase a specific real lead (`delete_lead` is used only for test leads in `admin.py`). Agent-side 12-month cleanup is still manual (CLAUDE.md pending 8).

**2.8 Transfers and region (MED).** Railway hosting is described as in the US (`legal.html` section 7); the app's Azure OpenAI resource region is not in the repo (unverified). Azure Data Zone EU deployments keep processing in the EU; Global deployments may not ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5544730/how-to-guarantee-eu-data-and-processing-residency), community source). Mitigation: EU region for Railway and Azure, and say so.

**2.9 AI Act Art. 50 (LOW-MED).** The chatbot disclosure duty applies from 2 Aug 2026 and was not delayed; only a generative-content marking grace period exists ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/08/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); law-firm commentary, not the OJ text). "Asistente virtual" (G13) is probably enough but never says "IA". Cheap fix: "asistente virtual (IA)". A DPIA is likely advisable (large-scale-ish profiling of non-customers with an AI, public-source data); unverified, ask the lawyer. Record of processing (Art. 30) and consent proof: `ack_at` stores only a timestamp and text version, not who or the exact text.

## 3. Third-party terms

**CASAFARI (MED).** Terms: non-sublicensable licence for the client's "internal business operations"; may not "pass or allow access to the API or API Data to any third party"; no AI/ML training without written permission; delete API Data on termination; CASAFARI acts as controller and the client must ensure "necessary appropriate consents and notices" ([data-supply terms](https://www.casafari.com/data-supply-terms-conditions/); summarized, no version date on page). One Sparrow key serving many agents conflicts with the third-party clause; one key per agent is the safe course. Feeding listing data into an LLM prompt is arguably fine but ask in writing.
**Google (LOW).** Limited Use applies to Sensitive/Restricted scopes: use only for user-facing features, no transfer except to provide the feature, no ads ([policy, 15 Feb 2024](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy)). The draft already says this; the calendar description carries lead data into the agent's own calendar, which is the agent's.
**Azure OpenAI.** Not trained on your data; abuse-monitoring review may occur (Microsoft staff answer, Feb 2026, unverified retention terms). The draft says Azure gets only slot lists for Google data but it receives owner chat text; align the wording.
